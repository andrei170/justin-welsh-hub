Copy Strategy - Justin Welsh

Prepared: 2026-05-16 For: Justin Welsh (@thejustinwelsh) Scope: Voice, pillars, headline bank, hero variants, the framing AG1 writes to across the VSL, the landing page, the cross-sell sequences, and the scorecard surface.

Voice profile

The voice is Justin's own, not AG1's. AG1 writes copy that lands inside Justin's existing register without imitating it. The register reads as:

  • Operator-grounded, not guru
  • Direct, short-sentence, anti-fluff
  • Math-aware (revenue, AOV, conversion-rate, list-size)
  • Solo-operator pride without machismo
  • Anti-corporate but not anti-business
  • Carries the burnt-out-corporate origin in the bones, never on the sleeve
  • Comfortable saying "no" - to hires, to ads, to scaling for its own sake
  • Comfortable with patience, multi-year compounding, weekly cadence

Words and phrases that fit: - "one-person business," "solo operator," "solopreneur" - "the math," "the run-rate," "the AOV," "the list," "the lane" - "built without ads," "Saturday morning," "weekly cadence" - "the audit," "the install," "the sequence," "the layer" - "stage one," "stage two," "stage three" - "the bridge," "the cross-sell," "the post-purchase" - "the productised tier," "the application tier," "the inquiry lane"

Words and phrases to avoid (off-voice on this prospect): - "transform," "unlock," "10x," "next level," "game-changer," "revolution" - "supercharge," "manifest," "abundance," "scale to the moon" - "team," "agency," "headcount," "hire your first VA" (off-mission for a $10M solo operator) - "secret," "hack," "blueprint" used as a buzzword - Em-dashes anywhere (the prospect's own voice is short-sentence, not aside-heavy) - Triple-adjective lists ("clear, compounding, simple") - "not just X, but Y" structures - Minute-CTAs ("book a 15-minute call" - off-brand for an operator-tier audience)

Brand pillars (the four things the copy keeps returning to)

Pillar 01 - One-person business is a discipline, not a constraint. The copy never apologises for solo. The copy treats solo as the operating principle. Anything that hires headcount or expands scope outside one-person operating is off-pillar.

Pillar 02 - Audience is a leading indicator, revenue is a lagging one, infrastructure connects the two. The copy speaks fluently about why 1M LinkedIn followers does not equal $1M revenue unless the funnel between the two is wired. This pillar gives the wedge its language.

Pillar 03 - Patience compounds. Cadence beats burst. Saturday Solopreneur is weekly for a reason. The Operating-System courses are evergreen for a reason. The copy treats compounding as the default, launches as the exception.

Pillar 04 - The asset is the audience, not the algorithm. Every funnel piece AG1 builds owns the relationship (email list, scorecard email-capture, application form). Nothing AG1 ships routes value into a platform Justin does not own.

Hero variants (landing page)

Four heroes. Each leads with the same underlying claim - "the bridge between the assets you already have" - written four ways for split-testing or for matching the surface (landing page vs scorecard reveal vs newsletter Operator Issue vs LinkedIn post).

Hero A - The bridge claim (default landing-page hero)

Three courses. A 175K newsletter. A 1M LinkedIn audience. The bridge between them is what gets installed.

Hero B - The math hero (data-led variant for the scorecard reveal page)

The 175K newsletter converts. The Creator MBA converts. The VSL bridge between them is the line that does not exist yet.

Hero C - The cross-sell hero (the LinkedIn / X drop-in for the cross-sell install reveal)

Buy LinkedIn OS. The Content OS is not in the order bump. Three products, three checkouts, no cross-sell. That is the line.

Hero D - The audit hero (the application-route hero for the high-score scorecard reveal)

You scored 80+. You are a stage-3 solopreneur. The next move is not another course. It is the audit.

Sentence rhythms (the copy rules AG1 writes to)

  • Short sentences default. Long sentences earn their place.
  • Paragraphs run 2-4 sentences. Paragraphs of 1 sentence get used for emphasis, not as default.
  • Numerals over words for numbers ("3 products" not "three products" in body copy where the number is the point; word form is fine in prose where it is not load-bearing).
  • "And" beats "as well as." "Also" beats "in addition." "Because" beats "owing to the fact that."
  • Active voice across body copy. Passive voice only in callouts where it shifts emphasis to the object.
  • One claim per paragraph. The supporting sentence specifies, it does not repeat.
  • The CTA is a sentence, not a phrase. "Apply to the next cohort" beats "Apply." "Watch the 7-minute Creator MBA preview" beats "Watch now."
  • Math gets named. "25-35% AOV lift" beats "significant AOV lift." "175K subscribers" beats "a large list."

Avatar fragments (who reads each surface, in their own words)

Stage 1 solopreneur (newsletter front-door avatar): - "I have 8K LinkedIn followers and a notion doc full of ideas. I do not have a product yet." - "I think I am a writer. I am not sure if I am a writer who sells things." - "I read Saturday Solopreneur every week. I have not bought anything."

Stage 2 solopreneur (Operating-System and Creator MBA avatar): - "I have 30K LinkedIn followers. The first course is selling. The second one I want to launch is not as polished." - "I built the audience. I am not sure what the second product is." - "I bought LinkedIn OS. I am wondering what is next."

Stage 3 solopreneur (application / 1-1 inquiry avatar): - "I have 100K+ LinkedIn followers. The course is selling. I am leaving money on the table." - "I do not want another course. I want someone to look at what I am running." - "I am a stage-3 solopreneur. There is no public route to ask you to look at it."

The avatars drive the routing on the scorecard. Each avatar gets a different post-score route.

Banned phrases on this prospect (the slop filter)

The copy never uses any of the following:

  • "Transform" or "transformational" (any variant)
  • "Unlock" or "unleash" (any variant)
  • "Game-changer" or "game-changing"
  • "10x" or "next level"
  • "Next-generation," "cutting-edge"
  • "Synergy," "ecosystem," "holistic"
  • "Manifest," "abundance"
  • "Disrupt," "pivot" (the operator-stage avatar treats these as red flags)
  • "Investment" (used as a euphemism for price - the copy says "price")
  • "Journey" (used as a euphemism for "process")

Em-dashes are excluded from every deliverable. Replacement punctuation is comma, full stop, or colon depending on context.
