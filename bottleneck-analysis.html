Three Things I Would Build First - Justin Welsh

Prepared: 2026-05-16 For: Justin Welsh (@thejustinwelsh) Context: Cold pitch. Justin runs a one-person business with three productised courses (LinkedIn Operating System, Content Operating System, Creator MBA Masterclass), the Saturday Solopreneur newsletter at 175K+ subscribers, and a cross-platform audience north of 1.8M weighted to LinkedIn (1M+) and X (~572K). The offers exist. The audience exists. The funnel infrastructure connecting the assets does not.

The frame

This is not "you need a new offer." Justin has three Kajabi-hosted courses (LinkedIn OS, Content OS, Creator MBA Masterclass) plus a 175K-subscriber Saturday Solopreneur newsletter plus a 1M+ LinkedIn audience plus a public sponsor inventory line. The offer surface is built.

The wedge sits in the gaps between the assets:

  1. The three Kajabi products run as isolated Stripe checkouts. No bundle, no order bump, no cross-sell flow, no post-purchase upsell.
  2. The 175K newsletter routes warm subscribers to the next Saturday issue. There is no VSL or webinar bridge into the Creator MBA, which is the highest-ticket productised offer on the public surface.
  3. The 1M+ LinkedIn audience routes to newsletter capture. There is no application form, no scorecard, no 1-1 inquiry lane for the operator-tier audience who wants more than a self-paced course.

AG1's pitch is the funnel infrastructure layer. Not a course. Not a cohort. Not a rebrand. The work is wiring, sequencing, and conversion-asset production around what Justin already runs.

Bottleneck 01 - The three Kajabi products are running as three isolated Stripe checkouts

The fact: LinkedIn Operating System sits at the self-paced course tier on Kajabi. Content Operating System sits at the same tier. Creator MBA Masterclass goes live as the third tier. Each product has its own checkout page. A LinkedIn OS buyer is not surfaced Content OS at the moment of purchase. A Content OS buyer is not surfaced LinkedIn OS. Neither is offered the Creator MBA Masterclass as the next tier up via a post-purchase sequence.

Why this is a bottleneck: The hardest part of a sale is acquiring the buyer. Justin has already done that work three times over (30,000+ LinkedIn OS students claimed, similar scale on Content OS, large pre-launch list on Creator MBA). The cross-sell layer adds revenue per existing buyer at near-zero acquisition cost. A 20-30% bundle bump at checkout on the second course, plus a 5-touch post-purchase sequence introducing the next tier up, is conservatively a 25-40% AOV lift on the two live products and the equivalent on Creator MBA at launch.

What AG1 actually builds for this: - Bundle pricing logic at Kajabi checkout (LinkedIn OS + Content OS at a bundled rate, both auto-cross-promote each other) - Order bump on each checkout for the complementary product - 5-touch post-purchase email sequence from LinkedIn OS buyer toward Content OS, and the inverse - 5-touch post-purchase email sequence from either course toward Creator MBA Masterclass when it launches - Kajabi automation tags wired against ConvertKit / native Kajabi email - Stripe-side integration confirmed working before any sequence fires

Year-1 floor math (conservative): A 25-35% AOV lift on the existing two live products plus the Creator MBA cross-sell at launch is the most conservative read. The combined revenue lift sits in the mid-six-figure range across the three-product portfolio in Year 1, scaling as Creator MBA enrolment volume builds. Lower bound assumes the two existing products at current run-rate plus a conservative Creator MBA launch month. Upper bound assumes Creator MBA hits its public pre-launch list size and the cross-sell layer compounds for a full 12 months.

Time to live: Day 1 to Day 3 of install. Kajabi + Stripe + ConvertKit (or native Kajabi email) all wired with documented playbacks. Test buys confirm sequences fire before the cross-sell goes live to the real list.

Bottleneck 02 - 175K Saturday Solopreneur newsletter and no VSL bridge into Creator MBA

The fact: The newsletter signup confirmation routes to the next Saturday issue. There is no VSL or webinar sitting between the newsletter and the Creator MBA Masterclass. The newsletter does the awareness and trust-building work; the Creator MBA does the conversion work; nothing bridges the two. When the newsletter promotes Creator MBA in a Saturday issue, the click goes to a static sales page on Kajabi, not to a VSL that warms the buyer before the checkout.

Why this is a bottleneck: A static sales page converts a warm email subscriber at one rate. A 6-9 minute VSL placed before the same sales page converts the same subscriber at a meaningfully higher rate. The number varies by audience, but for an operator audience like the Saturday Solopreneur readership, the lift is real and well-documented across creator-economy launches. The 175K subscriber list is the warmest possible audience for the Creator MBA, and the conversion surface between them is currently absent.

The CMBA2026FLASHSALE coupon code on the public homepage tells you the Creator MBA is closer to launch than the "Coming Soon" framing implies. The VSL bridge is the one piece that has to ship before launch, not after.

What AG1 actually builds for this: - 6-9 minute Creator MBA Masterclass VSL, scripted, recorded, edited - Dedicated landing page hosting the VSL, with checkout below - ConvertKit / Kajabi tag-triggered routing from newsletter signup confirmation into a 3-7-touch nurture sequence that ends at the VSL landing page - Saturday Solopreneur in-issue placement template for promoting Creator MBA into the VSL bridge (so Saturday issues stop sending traffic straight to the static page) - Tracking pixels and conversion events fire so the conversion-rate lift is measurable from day one - Evergreen replay sequence for subscribers who do not buy on first VSL view

Year-1 floor math (conservative): Conservative pickup on the bridged-VSL path versus the static-page path is a multi-percentage-point lift on the cohort of newsletter subscribers who reach the Creator MBA offer. On a 175K list with even modest Creator MBA launch flow, the resulting Year-1 revenue lift sits in the high-six-figure range, with most of the upside concentrated in the first 90 days post-launch. The number rises with launch list size and falls with subscriber-to-Creator-MBA-page conversion rate; both inputs are within reasonable defaults for a warm operator audience.

Time to live: Day 4 to Day 7 of install. VSL scripted Day 4 with Justin signing off. Recorded Day 5. Edited and landing page deployed Day 6. ConvertKit / Kajabi routing wired Day 7. Live before the next Saturday issue.

Bottleneck 03 - 1M+ LinkedIn audience and no scorecard or application route above the productised tier

The fact: The LinkedIn 1M+ audience routes to newsletter capture as the primary CTA. The newsletter routes to the productised courses ($200 tier) and, when it launches, the Creator MBA Masterclass. There is no scorecard. No quiz. No application form. No 1-1 inquiry route. The operator-tier reader who has read the newsletter for 18 months, completed both Operating System courses, and wants a higher-touch lane has nowhere to go.

Why this is a bottleneck: Two things are missing at once. First, the data: a scorecard returns a per-respondent readiness signal that pre-qualifies inbound for the right tier (newsletter, Creator MBA, or above). Second, the ceiling: the productised tier ($200 self-paced + Creator MBA Masterclass when live) is the public ceiling. There is no application-gated mentorship or 1-1 advisory lane for the audience segment with serious capital who specifically want Justin's eyes on their one-person business. That audience exists in a 1M+ LinkedIn following. They currently route nowhere.

The scorecard plus a 1-1 inquiry route above Creator MBA is the simplest possible way to surface and serve that demand without Justin having to build a new productised offer. Justin keeps his solo-operator model. The inquiry route runs by application, capped at a number Justin chooses, on whatever cadence respects his calendar.

What AG1 actually builds for this: - 12-question "Solopreneur Stage Scorecard" on ScoreApp or Typeform (current stage, audience size, revenue runrate, time invested per week, current funnel state, post-Creator-MBA bottleneck, etc) - 0-100 score plus a 3-line bottleneck readout returned in-browser - Email capture on the score reveal step - Routing logic - low-score routes to Saturday Solopreneur newsletter signup, mid-score routes to LinkedIn OS or Content OS, high-score (80+) routes to a Calendly for a 1-1 inquiry call - Linktree / LinkedIn bio / X bio / IG bio updates so the scorecard is the primary CTA next to newsletter signup - Application-form layer for the 1-1 inquiry route, with Justin's selection criteria documented so qualified inbound is the only thing that books

Year-1 floor math (conservative): Two revenue lines fire from this surface. First, the incremental newsletter and Operating-System-tier capture from the scorecard email-out (5-8% IG / LinkedIn-to-email is realistic on an operator audience at this scale, conservatively in the low-thousands of incremental subscribers in the first 90 days, compounding over the year). Second, the 1-1 inquiry route - even at a tightly capped 5-10 calls per quarter at a high-ticket price point, the line is six figures Year 1 on its own. The combined Year-1 floor sits in the mid-to-high-six-figure range, weighted toward the 1-1 line.

Time to live: Day 4 to Day 7 of install. Scorecard scripted Day 4. Deployed and tested Day 5. Bio routing updated Day 6. Application form and Calendly wired Day 7.

Combined Year-1 floor (the three lines together)

The three bottlenecks compound. Cross-sell adds revenue per existing buyer. The VSL bridge converts the warm list to Creator MBA at a higher rate. The scorecard and 1-1 lane open a tier above the productised ceiling.

Combined conservative Year-1 floor across the three lines: high-six-figures to low-seven-figures incremental on top of Justin's current run-rate, with the upside concentrated in the second half of Year 1 as the cross-sell compounds, the Creator MBA list matures, and the 1-1 lane fills.

Results vary, dependent on Creator MBA launch list size, current Kajabi pricing and conversion rates, Justin's input on the 1-1 lane cap, and the rate at which the cross-sell sequences compound. The ranges above assume conservative defaults across the inputs.

What is explicitly not in scope

  • AG1 does not build a new course, a new cohort, or a new productised offer. The three existing Kajabi products plus the Creator MBA Masterclass when it launches are the offer surface. AG1 wires the infrastructure around them.
  • AG1 does not run paid traffic in this engagement. Paid traffic is a future-phase service once the cross-sell, VSL, and scorecard surfaces are live. Justin's "built without ads" brand badge stays intact; future paid traffic, when it runs, runs against the existing on-brand offers, not against cold strangers.
  • AG1 does not touch the Saturday Solopreneur editorial. The newsletter writing, the topic selection, the voice are Justin's. AG1 builds the routing and sequencing on top of the newsletter; the newsletter itself is untouched.
  • AG1 does not migrate Kajabi to a different platform. Cross-sell, bundle, automation, and tagging all get wired inside Kajabi as it is.
